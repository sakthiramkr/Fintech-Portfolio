Product Influence · VoC
From Customer Signals to Roadmap Prioritisation

How aggregating signals across four data sources surfaced a product gap, built a business case, and moved a feature from backlog to beta in one quarter.

4-Source Signal Aggregation Business Case Development Roadmap Influence Enterprise Deal Recovery

TrainerCentral's pipeline was growing but late-stage enterprise deals kept stalling — and no one had a systematic view of why. I built a signal aggregation engine pulling from four sources simultaneously: G2 and Capterra review patterns, Reddit community discussions, support ticket themes, and CRM lost-deal notes.

A clear pattern emerged. Enterprise buyers were consistently flagging SCORM compliance as a blocker. Without SCORM, content migration from existing authoring tools was impossible — and every major competitor had it. The signal was strong enough across all four sources to quantify: multiple six-figure opportunities were stalled or lost against this single gap.

I compiled a business case — competitive analysis, cross-source signal volume, blocked pipeline estimate, and a phased rollout proposal — and presented to the product team. SCORM was prioritised within one quarter and shipped to beta, deployed first to the accounts that had flagged it directly.

Method
4-source aggregation — G2, Capterra, support tickets, CRM lost-deal notes
Insight
Single product gap identified as consistent signal across all four sources
Business Case
Competitive analysis + signal volume + blocked pipeline value
Outcome
Feature prioritised and shipped to beta in one quarter

Why this transfers to payments: The same signal aggregation method applies directly to competitive categories like merchant acquiring — where product gaps (missing payment methods, slow settlement, poor reconciliation UX) are often the real deal-killers that never surface in structured win/loss calls. The method, not the domain, is the skill.

Pricing Strategy · Revenue
Pricing Architecture Through Cohort Testing and Customer Research

How structured research, willingness-to-pay analysis, and cohort testing rebuilt a flat pricing model into a tiered architecture — delivering an 18% revenue lift over two quarters.

18% Revenue Lift Cohort Testing Enterprise Tier Design Willingness-to-Pay Research

The original flat pricing had no feature caps — power users sat on entry-level plans with no natural reason to upgrade. I started with structured research: customer interviews to map real usage patterns, CRM usage data to find where customers were outgrowing their plans, and a competitor pricing teardown to understand where we were undercharging.

The output was a pricing architecture with three deliberate interventions. First, feature caps at key usage thresholds created clear value gaps between tiers. Second, a new mid-tier plan was designed and cohort-tested with existing customers before full rollout — their feedback directly shaped which caps to set and which features to hold back. Third, an enterprise-adjacent plan was designed with exclusive capabilities (SAML SSO, field-level encryption, advanced analytics) — used as a conversation tool in late-stage deals before public launch.

Revenue lifted 18% across two quarters post-rollout, driven by new conversions to mid-tier and upgrade pressure on existing power users who had outgrown the entry plan.

Revenue Impact
18% lift over 2 quarters post-rollout
Method
Cohort-tested with existing customers before full launch
Enterprise Tier
SSO, encryption, analytics — used in late-stage deal conversations
Inputs
Customer interviews + CRM usage signals + competitor pricing teardown

Why this transfers to payments: Pricing strategy in merchant acquiring — MDR structures, interchange-plus models, flat-rate vs. tiered plans, per-transaction fees — follows the same principles: segment willingness-to-pay, competitive anchoring, and feature-gating to drive upgrade behaviour. The mechanics are directly applicable.

Competitive Intelligence · AI
AI-Powered Competitive Intelligence Tracker

A production-grade market intelligence system built using the Claude API — no vendor tool, no off-the-shelf solution. Surfaces deal-blocking competitive signals in real time.

Claude API Real-time Signal Monitoring Battlecard Automation Deal Velocity Recovery

Competitor pages are monitored for changes via VisualPing and Distill.io, routed alongside Google Alerts and Feedly signals into a structured log. Claude analyses each signal, classifies it by theme and urgency, detects patterns across weeks, and surfaces actionable recommendations — keeping campaign messaging and positioning current without manual research cycles.

The system was built to solve a specific problem: sales reps were walking into enterprise deals with stale battlecards, and losing to objections we hadn't anticipated. By routing CI output directly into battlecard updates and sales talk tracks, the system eliminated the lag between a competitor's move and our field response.
